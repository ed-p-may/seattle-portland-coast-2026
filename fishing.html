Late September, specifically

What is actually running

The seasonal headline is salmon. Trout is a plausible secondary conversation; winter steelhead is not.

OR / 01

Fall Chinook

Chinook begin entering Tillamook Bay in September, with October more often the peak. Late September is credible but early enough that tide, rain, and guide judgment matter.

OR / 02

Coho on Saturday

In 2026, wild coho may be retained in the Nehalem, Tillamook, and Nestucca basins September 9–October 28 on Wednesdays, Saturdays, and Sundays only: one per day and three for the season.

OR / 03

Coastal cutthroat

Resident and sea-run cutthroat occupy north-coast streams, with sea-runs returning in late summer and early fall. Ask whether a trout add-on is realistic; do not dilute the prime salmon window just to check a species box.

OR / 04

Not steelhead season

North-coast winter steelhead is a winter fishery. A guide page labeled “steelhead” may describe the right operator or river, but it is not the right September target.

WA / 05

Puget Sound coho

Ocean-run coho return to Seattle waters from mid-August into October. It is the local late-September specialty and the logical target if Tuesday becomes a fishing morning.

ALL / 06

No catch guarantee

A legal season is not a forecast. Ask each guide what they have caught in the previous seven days, where, and whether they would still choose the proposed protected water for this exact date.

Before paying a deposit

  • Get the exact river/bay, launch, boat type, start/end time, passenger count, and all-in price in writing.
  • Confirm rods, tackle, bait, PFDs, rain gear, cleaning/filleting, and gratuity expectations.
  • Ask the guide exactly which Oregon license and harvest tag each angler needs. The 2026 one-day angling/shellfish license is $29; buy only after the water is confirmed.
  • Clarify padded seating, standing time, step height into the boat, nearby restroom stops, food/water, and what happens if wind or ocean conditions change the plan.
  • Decide before fishing whether a retained catch becomes Saturday dinner, is released, or travels home. Cleaning/filleting is not the same as vacuum-packing and freezing.

If fish is coming home

Call a processor before the trip

Captain’s Corner serves the Garibaldi fleet and advertises seafood processing. Ask in advance whether they will take sport-caught salmon on Saturday, vacuum-pack and freeze it in time for Sunday departure, and supply a travel box. Then confirm the airline’s frozen-fish and cooler rules.
