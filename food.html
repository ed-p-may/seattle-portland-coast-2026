Field notes · September 21–28

Eat the route.

Oysters at the farm. Seattle teriyaki. A last-of-the-season coast market. Smoked fish, tiny-batch chowder, fresh albacore, and one genuinely special dinner.

Useful, local, and worth the stop—without turning the trip into a restaurant endurance test.

Grillbird

Order: chicken or salmon teriyaki; add mac salad or Spam musubi.

Seattle-style teriyaki is local comfort food: sweet-savory marinated meat, grilled hard enough to char, with rice and salad. Grillbird is a polished neighborhood version, open every day 11 a.m.–9 p.m., with its own parking lot.

Walk-in counter service, free parking, and a broad view back across Elliott Bay. It is a good first-day lunch or early dinner when “sit outside near water” sounds better than a formal meal. Open Monday–Thursday 11 a.m.–9 p.m.

This is the farm, not a themed oyster bar: first-come waterfront picnic tables, trays of freshly shucked oysters, grilled shellfish, and a daily seasonal menu. Open daily 11 a.m.–7:30 p.m. Parking is limited; arrive at opening on Tuesday.

Pocket tasting list

What to look for in September

Ask “what came in locally?” before ordering by species. Menus move faster than websites on the coast.

WA / 01

Olympia + Shigoku oysters

Olympias are Washington’s tiny native oyster; Shigokus are Pacific oysters tumbled in bags for a deep cup and crisp finish. A side-by-side tasting is more interesting than a giant platter.

WA / 02

Geoduck

Pronounced “gooey-duck.” This huge local clam is usually served raw or very lightly cooked. Try a few slices if it appears; there is no need to organize a whole meal around it.

OR / 03

Fresh albacore

Oregon’s hook-and-line albacore season runs into September and often October. Look for tuna melts, smoked tuna, or simply grilled loin—and ask whether it is this season’s coast catch.

OR / 04

Fall salmon

The Tillamook basin’s September story is Chinook and coho. Seek smoked salmon, salmon cakes, or a simply cooked fillet; save elaborate sauces for a less distinctive fish.

OR / 05

Dungeness crab

September is when bay crabs begin to fill out after the late-summer molt, and recreational bay crabbing is open year-round. At a restaurant or market, still ask whether the crab is local and fresh.

OR / 06

Marionberry + hazelnut

Fresh marionberries are past peak by late September, so look for jam, pie, or syrup. Hazelnut harvest begins around this time—roasted nuts, brittle, and hazelnut pastries travel well.

Good things to carry home

Smoked albacore · smoked salmon · canned local tuna · marionberry preserves · roasted Oregon hazelnuts · aged Tillamook cheddar · Jacobsen sea salt

Freshness notes

Hours, event dates, and current menus were checked against official sources on September 2, 2026. Coast businesses are small and seasonal: call before making a special drive, especially for Buttercup, the smokehouse, seafood counters, or weather-dependent service.

For live seafood conditions and harvest—not restaurant meals—check ODFW before crabbing or shellfishing. Razor-clam harvest north of Tillamook Head is under its annual conservation closure through September 30.
