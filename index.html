Tuesday’s fork in the road

Choose one excellent day.

Both options begin at 5 Spot and end at Il Nido. The middle is the only decision.

A

Samish Bay oysters

Drive 75–90 minutes north for Taylor Shellfish at opening: a working shellfish farm, waterfront tables, and a deeply Pacific Northwest lunch.

  • Best for food and scenery
  • About three hours total driving
  • Return directly for an afternoon rest
Taylor Shellfish Samish ↗

B

Working waterfront

Stay in Seattle for the Ballard Locks and fish ladder, followed by lunch at Fishermen’s Terminal. Edmonds Pier remains the fishing/weather backup.

  • Best for infrastructure and maritime history
  • Much less driving
  • Easier afternoon rest
Ballard Locks ↗

Current lean: Samish Bay if several people are excited about oysters; otherwise Ballard.

How this trip works

Good pacing is part of the plan.

01

One anchor per half-day

Optional stops disappear before meals, rest, or the main event does.

02

Stop before anyone needs to

Plan a movement and restroom break every 60–90 minutes and a small food opportunity every 2–3 hours.

03

Beat the crowd

Use opening times, weekday visits, and early Cannon Beach rather than peak periods.

04

Let weather win

Coastal outdoor plans remain flexible until the preceding week. The museum is the excellent rain plan.

Useful “don’ts”

We are not collecting attractions.

  • Don’t combine Samish Bay with Ballard Locks.
  • Don’t visit Pike Place at peak hours just because it is famous.
  • Don’t add the Space Needle / Chihuly / MoPOP cluster without a personal request.
  • Don’t book an Underground tour: too much standing, stairs, and crowding.
  • Don’t turn Cannon Beach into a long Sunday stop.
  • Don’t add Ecola or Oswald West hikes to this itinerary.
  • Don’t attempt Coffenbury Lake, Cannon Beach, and the full Astoria museum on Sunday.
